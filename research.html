Anal cancer screening

We study strategies to improve the implementation of evidence-based cancer screening and prevention, with a particular focus on anal cancer screening among high-risk populations. Our work addresses barriers to screening, integration into routine clinical care, stigma reduction, and follow-up treatment to improve prevention and early detection.

  • Improve screening among high-risk populations
  • Integrate anal cancer screening into routine HIV care
  • Develop stigma-responsive screening strategies
  • Improve treatment adherence for anal precancerous lesions

Current project

Understanding the decisional needs for anal cancer screening among high-risk populations

Understanding screening knowledge, values, preferences, and decision-making needs across four populations at elevated risk for anal cancer, to inform patient-centered decision aids.

Convergent mixed methods · Ottawa Decision Support Framework

Project overview and methods

This convergent mixed-methods study aims to understand the decisional needs of four populations at elevated risk for anal cancer: people with HIV (PWH), men who have sex with men (MSM), individuals with a history of HPV-related gynecologic malignancies or high-grade dysplasia, and solid organ transplant recipients. Guided by the Ottawa Decision Support Framework, the study explores participants’ knowledge of anal cancer and screening, perceived risk, personal values, preferences, social support, and decisional conflict. Although anal cancer screening is increasingly recommended for high-risk populations, barriers such as limited awareness, stigma, and uncertainty regarding screening benefits and potential harms complicate decision-making. The findings provide a foundation for developing patient-centered decision aids that support informed, value-concordant decisions regarding anal cancer screening.

The study employs a convergent mixed-methods design, collecting quantitative survey data and qualitative interview data in parallel. Approximately 260 participants complete a brief survey assessing sociodemographic characteristics, screening knowledge, decision stage, values, social support, decisional conflict, and preferences for a future decision aid. A purposive subset of approximately 60 participants participates in semi-structured interviews exploring their experiences, knowledge gaps, decision-making challenges, and perspectives on screening, with additional attention to population-specific concerns. Survey data are analyzed using descriptive statistics, while interview transcripts undergo thematic analysis guided by the Ottawa Decision Support Framework. Findings are integrated at the aggregate level to provide a comprehensive understanding of decisional needs across the four populations. The results directly inform the content, structure, framing, and delivery format of future anal cancer screening decision aid prototypes.

Current project

Understanding barriers to anal precancer treatment

Examining patient and healthcare provider perspectives on barriers to completing recommended treatment for anal high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions, with the aim of informing targeted implementation strategies.

Convergent mixed methods · COM-B model · Behavior Change Wheel

Project overview and methods

This convergent mixed-methods study aims to identify barriers that prevent patients diagnosed with anal high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (HSIL), a precancerous condition, from completing recommended treatment. Guided by the Capability, Opportunity, Motivation–Behavior (COM-B) model and the Behavior Change Wheel (BCW), the study explores patient and healthcare provider perspectives on factors influencing treatment adherence. Despite evidence demonstrating that treatment of anal HSIL significantly reduces the risk of progression to anal cancer, adherence to recommended treatment and follow-up remains suboptimal. The study examines individual, healthcare-related, and contextual barriers to treatment completion, as well as potential strategies to improve patient engagement and retention in care. Findings will inform the development of targeted strategies to improve adherence to anal precancer treatment and reduce the risk of progression to invasive anal cancer.

The study employs a convergent mixed-methods design, collecting quantitative survey data and qualitative interview data in parallel. Approximately 35 patients with confirmed anal HSIL and 10 healthcare providers involved in anal HSIL care complete brief electronic surveys assessing treatment completion, perceived barriers to adherence, and potential strategies for improvement. A purposive subset of approximately 10 patients who have not initiated recommended treatment within 90 days of diagnosis and five healthcare providers participate in semi-structured interviews to further explore their experiences and perspectives. Survey data are analyzed using descriptive statistics, while interview transcripts undergo reflective thematic analysis guided by the COM-B model. Findings from the patient and provider surveys and interviews are integrated during analysis to provide a comprehensive understanding of barriers to treatment adherence across individual and healthcare system levels. The results will inform the development of targeted interventions and strategies to improve adherence to recommended anal precancer treatment and support timely, appropriate care.

Current project

Building a living, ontology-informed and large language model-assisted knowledge base for cancer screening implementation

Developing a continuously updated, structured resource that helps researchers and healthcare professionals find and compare cancer screening implementation strategies for their settings.

Evidence synthesis · Ontology engineering · Human–LLM collaboration

Project overview and methods

This project aims to develop a living, ontology-informed, and large language model (LLM)-assisted knowledge base to improve the accessibility, organization, and application of evidence on cancer screening implementation. Although numerous implementation strategies have been evaluated to improve cancer screening uptake, the evidence remains fragmented, inconsistently reported, and difficult to translate into clinical practice. The project addresses this gap by creating a continuously updated, structured, and computer-readable resource that organizes implementation evidence across cancer types, populations, and healthcare contexts. The knowledge base supports evidence-informed decision-making by enabling researchers and healthcare professionals to identify, compare, and retrieve implementation strategies that are relevant to their specific clinical and organizational settings.

The project employs a human-LLM collaborative workflow to develop and maintain the knowledge base. The research team develops a cancer screening implementation ontology, building on the Behavior Change Intervention Ontology, to standardize the representation of screening pathways, implementation determinants, strategies, and outcomes. Guided by Cochrane Effective Practice and Organization of Care methods, the team systematically identifies randomized controlled trials evaluating cancer screening implementation strategies across PubMed, EMBASE, CINAHL, and PsycINFO. Eligible studies undergo structured data extraction and standardization using the ontology, with LLM-assisted processes supported by source verification and expert review. The resulting interactive knowledge base enables evidence visualization, synthesis, and structured comparison, while ongoing updates and quality evaluations support its continued development. Ultimately, this resource provides an evidence infrastructure for advancing precision implementation science and developing future context-sensitive decision-support applications.

Cancer screening policy analysis

We examine how cancer screening policies are implemented across healthcare settings and populations. Using publicly available datasets, we identify implementation gaps, evaluate patterns in screening delivery, and generate evidence to inform policy and improve access to recommended screening services.

Supportive cancer care

We investigate strategies to improve the delivery of evidence-based supportive care in oncology. Our work focuses on improving uptake of genetic counseling and testing in community oncology and implementing serious illness conversations in geriatric oncology to support patient-centered care.

Project focus

Genetic counseling and testing in community oncology

Assess the landscape, identify implementation barriers, co-design strategies, and develop an implementation blueprint to improve uptake.

Project focus

Serious illness conversations in geriatric oncology

Study the implementation of serious illness conversations through a hybrid effectiveness–implementation type 1 trial.
